Do I need a deed poll after marriage?

Usually not. If your new name comes from the names on your marriage or civil partnership certificate, the certificate is the evidence you need. A deed poll is only for changes the certificate can't explain.

When your certificate is enough

  • Taking your partner's surname.
  • Double-barrelling your surname with your partner's, with or without a hyphen. More on double-barrelled names.
  • Your partner taking your surname. The rules are the same whoever changes. More on husbands taking their wife's name.
  • Going back to your own surname later, in most cases, with your certificate and evidence of your earlier name, such as your birth certificate.

There's no time limit. Your certificate works just as well years after the wedding. Changing your name years later.

When you need a deed poll

  • A new combined surname, for example Smith and Jones becoming Smones.
  • Changing your first name or middle names at the same time. Changing your first or middle name.
  • Moving your own surname to a middle name, which some organisations will ask a deed poll for.
  • A spelling that differs from your certificate.
  • A surname that isn't on your certificate at all, such as a family name from a grandparent.

If one of these applies to you, see our deed poll page for how to get one that organisations will accept.

Why your certificate works, even though your new name isn't on it

Your certificate records the names you married in, not the name you'll use next. That confuses a lot of people. It works because it proves the reason for the change: in the UK, taking your spouse's surname after marriage is accepted by custom, so organisations take the certificate as evidence. A deed poll is needed when your new name goes beyond what that custom covers.

Unenrolled and enrolled deed polls

In England and Wales there are two kinds:

  • Unenrolled. If you're 16 or over, you can make one yourself using the wording on GOV.UK, and use it straight away. You sign it in front of 2 witnesses aged 18 or over. Some organisations won't accept it if a witness lives with you or is a close relative.
  • Enrolled. If you're 18 or over, you can put your name change on public record with the High Court. It takes several months and your new name is published in The Gazette. A few organisations, such as some banks, mobile phone companies and energy providers, only accept an enrolled deed poll.

Most people changing their name after marriage never need either. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own processes: in Scotland, see National Records of Scotland, and in Northern Ireland, nidirect.

Before you get one

  • Check you need it. If you're simply taking your partner's surname, you don't.
  • Get the spelling right. Use exactly the same name, with the same hyphen or space, everywhere.
  • Do your passport early. Once your passport is in your new name, most organisations accept it as proof, so you'll post your deed poll fewer times.
  • Remember the government is free to deal with. HM Passport Office, DVLA and HMRC don't charge to update your name (a new passport has its usual fee). No private deed poll seller is part of the government.
