Guides

Do I need a deed poll after marriage?

Usually not. If your new name comes from the names on your marriage or civil partnership certificate, the certificate is the evidence you need.

When your certificate is enough

  • Taking your partner's surname.
  • Double-barrelling your surname with your partner's, with or without a hyphen. More on double-barrelled names.
  • Going back to your own surname later, in most cases, with your certificate and evidence of your earlier name.

When you need a deed poll

  • A new combined surname, for example Smith and Jones becoming Smones.
  • Changing your first name or middle names at the same time.
  • Moving your own surname to a middle name, which some organisations will ask a deed poll for.
  • A spelling that differs from your certificate.

You can make a deed poll yourself, for free

In England, Wales and Northern Ireland, you can write your own unenrolled deed poll for free and sign it in front of a witness. Most organisations, including HM Passport Office and DVLA, accept one. You only need to "enrol" a deed poll with the courts, for a fee, if you want your new name on public record. See gov.uk/change-name-deed-poll.

Scotland has its own process. If you live in Scotland, check with National Records of Scotland.

Our deed poll add-on

If you'd rather not write it yourself, we can prepare an unenrolled deed poll for you for £19, ready to sign in front of a witness. It's an add-on to any plan and included in Concierge. It's available in England and Wales. We don't enrol deed polls with the courts, and you don't need a solicitor for an unenrolled one. See prices.

Be careful with paid deed poll sites

Many websites sell deed polls for marriage name changes. If you're simply taking your partner's surname, you don't need one, and if you do need one you can write it yourself. No private deed poll seller is part of the government.
