Do I need a deed poll to go back to my maiden name?

Short answer: usually not, once your divorce is final.

When you don't need one

If your divorce is final and you're going back to a surname you had before, such as your birth surname, you can normally use:

  • your final order (or decree absolute)
  • your marriage certificate
  • your birth certificate

Together, they show the name you were born with, the name you took on marriage, and that the marriage has ended. That's what banks, employers and the government need to see.

When you do need one

  • Your divorce isn't final yet, and you want to change now. Until the final order, a deed poll is your proof.
  • You want a name you've never had, such as a new surname, or your mum's surname rather than the one on your birth certificate.
  • You're going back to a name from an earlier marriage, and you can't show the full paper trail. You may need one.
  • You want to change your first or middle names too.
  • An organisation insists on one. A few banks and phone companies do. Usually your new passport sorts this out instead.

See our deed poll page if one of these applies.

Why people are confused

Ask on any forum and you'll get two answers: "do a free one, DON'T pay" and "it costs about £50". Both can be true. In England and Wales you can make an unenrolled deed poll yourself, signed in front of 2 witnesses aged 18 or over. An enrolled one, recorded with the High Court, costs £53.05 and takes months. Most people going back to their name after a final divorce need neither.

If you'd rather use a deed poll, perhaps because it's simpler than tracking down your marriage and birth certificates, we can prepare one for you. It's printed on heavy paper and laid out the way banks and government offices expect to see it. And it makes a lovely keepsake of a big moment in your life. See our deed poll page for what's included.

The one thing to remember

Even without a deed poll, the order matters. Change your employer and council first, then your bank, then your passport. The Passport Office wants proof you're already using your name.
