All your name change questions, answered

My partner and I are getting married, the second time for both of us. When we talked about a joint family name, we realised that neither of us had finished changing our previous surnames everywhere. Not because it's difficult, it's just long and messy. Every organisation wanted something slightly different, and the advice online sometimes contradicted itself. So here are straight answers to the questions I hear most.

1. How many copies of my marriage certificate do I need?

My recommendation is getting three on the day. Your passport application keeps your certificate for several weeks, and you'll want to update your bank, your employer and others in the meantime. You can also get another copy from the General Register Office, which costs £12.50.

2. Do I need a deed poll to take my spouse's surname?

No. If you're simply taking your husband's or wife's surname, your marriage certificate is all the proof you need. You need a deed poll if you're doing something the certificate can't show, like changing a first or middle name, or making up a new surname together. If that's you, see our deed poll page.

3. What order should I do things in?

Passport first if you can, because once you have it, other places get easier. DVLA, for example, can use your new passport number instead of your certificate. After that: HMRC, your employer, the electoral register and benefits; then banks, pensions and savings; then everything else, from your GP to your gym. This free checklist puts this in order for you.

4. Which name do I book my honeymoon in?

The name on the passport you'll travel with. The airline ticket must match your passport exactly. If you want to travel in your new name, you can apply for a new passport up to three months before the wedding using form PD2, signed by your registrar. But your old passport is cancelled when you apply, so most couples simply book in their current name, which is what I would recommend. More in our honeymoon guide.

5. Will they accept a photocopy?

Usually not. Most organisations want the original certificate or a certified copy from the register office. A few, such as some building societies dealing by post, ask for a copy certified by a solicitor instead. This is the main reason to order spare copies.

6. Is there a time limit?

No. You can change your name the week after your wedding or ten years later, using the same marriage certificate. Plenty of people start, stall, and pick it up again when a passport is due for renewal.

7. Can I double-barrel with just my marriage certificate?

Often, yes. HM Passport Office accepts a marriage certificate for a double-barrelled surname made from both your names. Some banks and utility companies are stricter and may ask for a deed poll, so it helps to do your passport first and use that as proof. More on double-barrelling.

8. Why do I send my certificate when my new name isn't on it?

Your certificate records the names you married in, not the one you'll use next. It's proof of the reason for the change. In the UK, taking your spouse's surname after marriage is accepted by custom, so the certificate is enough.

9. Can I change my first or middle name at the same time?

Not with your marriage certificate alone. A first or middle name change needs a deed poll. See our deed poll page.

10. Can I keep my old name at work?

Yes. Many people keep their name professionally and use their married name elsewhere. Just be consistent: your payroll, HMRC record and pension need to match the name on your ID, even if your email signature doesn't. Keeping your maiden name, or using it at work.

11. Do I have to change my name at all?

No. There is no legal requirement to change your name when you marry, for anyone. In a February 2026 Opinium poll, nearly one in five women who changed their name said they wouldn't do it if they married now.

12. Can my husband take my name?

Yes, and the process is the same for him. A man taking his wife's surname can use the marriage certificate just as a woman would. Most organisations don't ask why.

Live in Scotland or Northern Ireland? Some steps differ, especially for deed polls. The checklist asks where you live and adjusts.
