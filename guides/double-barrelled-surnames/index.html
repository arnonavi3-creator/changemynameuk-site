Guides

Double-barrelled and combined surnames after marriage

If your double-barrelled name is made from both your surnames as they appear on your certificate, your certificate is usually enough. A brand-new combined name needs a deed poll.

Usually fine with your certificate

  • Smith-Jones or Jones-Smith, with a hyphen.
  • Smith Jones, without a hyphen.

Decide on the order and whether to use a hyphen before you start, and use exactly the same version everywhere. Mismatched spellings between your passport, licence and bank are a common cause of problems.

Needs a deed poll

  • A blended or "meshed" name, such as Smith and Jones becoming Smones.
  • A name that drops or changes letters from either surname.
  • Adding a surname that isn't on your certificate.

You can write a deed poll yourself, for free. Read our deed poll guide.

Can you both double-barrel?

Yes. Both partners can take the same double-barrelled name, using the same certificate as evidence. Each of you updates your own records.

Practical tips

  • Some online forms limit the number of characters or don't accept hyphens. Ask the organisation how they'll record it.
  • Tell organisations which part is your surname if they ask for a "last name" only.
