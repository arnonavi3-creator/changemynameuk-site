Double-barrelled and combined surnames after marriage

If your double-barrelled name is made from both your surnames as they appear on your certificate, your certificate is usually enough. A brand-new combined name needs a deed poll.

Usually fine with your certificate

  • Smith-Jones or Jones-Smith, with a hyphen.
  • Smith Jones, without a hyphen.

HM Passport Office accepts a marriage or civil partnership certificate for a double-barrelled surname made from both your names. Decide on the order and whether to use a hyphen before you start, and use exactly the same version everywhere. Mismatched spellings between your passport, licence and bank are a common cause of problems.

Needs a deed poll

  • A blended or "meshed" name, such as Smith and Jones becoming Smones.
  • A name that drops or changes letters from either surname.
  • Adding a surname that isn't on your certificate.

See our deed poll page if one of these is you, or read our guide to when you need a deed poll.

Do your passport first

Some banks, phone and energy companies are stricter about double-barrelled names than the Passport Office, and may ask for a deed poll even when your certificate shows both surnames. The simplest way round it is to get your passport in your new name first. Once you have it, most organisations accept your passport as proof, and the question doesn't come up.

Hyphen or no hyphen?

Both are fine, but they behave differently:

  • With a hyphen (Smith-Jones), systems tend to keep both parts together as one surname. Some online forms strip the hyphen or reject it, so check how your name has been recorded.
  • Without a hyphen (Smith Jones), some systems will treat Smith as a middle name and Jones as your surname. That can cause mismatches later, for example when an airline ticket and a passport disagree.

If you're unsure, a hyphen is usually the less confusing choice.

Which order?

There's no rule. Some couples put the name they want to pass on last, because many systems file people by the final part. Others go for whatever sounds best. Say it out loud with your first name, write it on a form, and imagine spelling it down the phone.

Can you both double-barrel?

Yes. Both partners can take the same double-barrelled name, using the same certificate as evidence. Each of you updates your own records. It's increasingly common, and it means you share a name without either of you giving yours up. If your partner is changing their name too, read our guide for husbands changing their name.

Children

Children born after you double-barrel can be registered with the double-barrelled name. Changing an existing child's surname is a separate process with its own rules, and usually needs the agreement of everyone with parental responsibility.

Practical tips

  • Some online forms limit the number of characters or don't accept hyphens. Ask the organisation how they'll record it.
  • Tell organisations which part is your surname if they ask for a "last name" only.
  • Keep a note of which accounts you've updated. It's easy to lose track. Our free checklist keeps the list for you.
