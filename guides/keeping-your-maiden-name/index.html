Guides

Keeping your maiden name, or using it at work

You don't have to change your name when you marry. In the UK you're free to keep your name, change it, or use different names in different places.

Keeping your name

If you keep your name, nothing changes and you don't need to tell anyone. You can still be known socially by your partner's surname.

Using your maiden name at work

Many people change their legal name but keep their old name professionally, for example as a doctor, lawyer or author. That's allowed. Your payroll, pension and tax records must use your legal name, and your employer may show your professional name elsewhere. Professional bodies have their own rules, so check with yours.

Moving your surname to a middle name

Some people keep their own surname as a middle name and take their partner's as their surname. Because your certificate doesn't show that name, some organisations will ask for a deed poll. Read more.

Changing back later

If you change your name and later want to go back, you can usually do it with your certificate and evidence of your earlier name, such as your birth certificate.
