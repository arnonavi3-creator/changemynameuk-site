Change your name yourself, for free

You don't need to pay anyone to change your name after a marriage or civil partnership. Here's how to do it yourself, using your certificate. The only government fee is for a new passport.

1. Decide on your new name

Taking your partner's surname, or joining both surnames, usually only needs your marriage or civil partnership certificate. A new combined name, or changing your first name, needs a deed poll, which you can write yourself for free. More on deed polls.

2. Sort out your passport

Apply for a new passport in your new name at gov.uk/changing-passport-information. An adult passport costs £102 online or £115.50 by post. If you're travelling soon after the wedding, read our honeymoon passport guide before you book or apply.

3. Update your driving licence and log book

Changing the name on your licence is free and you must do it by law. Use form D1 by post, as explained at gov.uk/change-name-driving-licence. If you own a car, update your V5C log book too. Our driving licence guide.

4. Tell HMRC

Update your name in your HMRC online account or the HMRC app. See gov.uk/tell-hmrc-change-of-details. Your National Insurance number stays the same.

5. Update the electoral register

Register again with your new name at gov.uk/register-to-vote. Banks and lenders use the register to check who you are.

6. Tell everyone else

Your GP, employer, bank, pension, insurers, utilities, council tax, mobile provider and more. We've written a free checklist of who to tell.
