Find your route

What you'll need depends on where you are

Pick the one that sounds most like you. There's no wrong answer, and you can go back to your name at any time.

Divorce or dissolution final

You have your final order

You usually don't need a deed poll. Your final order (or decree absolute), marriage or civil partnership certificate and birth certificate are normally enough.

Separated, or still going through it

Not divorced yet

You can still change your name now. You'll need a deed poll as your proof, which you can make yourself for free with two witnesses.

After losing a partner

Widowed

Take it gently. Many places accept your birth and marriage certificates with the death certificate. Some ask for a deed poll instead. Your checklist will help you see which.

Any other reason

It just feels right

You don't need anyone's permission or a reason. A deed poll is usually the proof organisations ask for, and it's free to make your own.

The order that works

Going back works the other way round

After a wedding, the passport often comes first. When you go back to a previous name, the Passport Office wants to see that you're already using it. So start small and let your proof build up.

  1. 1Start using your name

    Tell your employer and your council first. A payslip or council letter in your name becomes your proof.

  2. 2Then your bank

    Most banks accept your final order with your marriage and birth certificates, or your deed poll.

  3. 3Then your passport

    Send a signed statement that you've gone back to your name "for all purposes", plus proof you're using it.

  4. 4Then everything else

    DVLA, HMRC, your GP and dentist, pensions, insurance, utilities and the ones you forgot about.

Worth knowing

A few things people worry about

A different surname from your children

It's legal, common, and schools and GPs deal with it every day. When you travel abroad without their other parent, carry their birth certificate and a consent letter.

Keeping your married name is fine too

Lots of people keep it to match their children or because their work is known by it. That's a real choice, and there's no time limit if you change your mind later.

Mrs, Ms or Mx

Your title isn't part of your legal name. You can change it whenever you like, for free. Just tell the places that use it.

Questions

Do I need a deed poll to go back to my maiden name?

Not usually, once your divorce or dissolution is final. Your final order, marriage or civil partnership certificate and birth certificate are normally enough. If you're separated but not yet divorced, or want a name you've never had, you'll need a deed poll. More on deed polls.

Why can't I start with my passport?

HM Passport Office asks for evidence that you're already using your previous name, such as a payslip or a letter from your council, plus a signed statement that you've gone back to it for all purposes. So update your employer and council first. See GOV.UK.

Is there a time limit?

No. You can go back to your name the month after your divorce or twenty years later.

Can I go back to a name from an earlier marriage?

Usually yes. You'll need the documents that link each name, so it can help to gather your certificates before you start. Your checklist will flag it.

I've lost my marriage certificate. What now?

You can order a copy from the register office where you married, or from the General Register Office.

How long does it take?

Most places update within days or a few weeks. The passport takes longest. With a full list and the right order, most people finish in a couple of months.
