Why it keeps happening

It's not you. It's 13 years of paperwork.

The typical marriage that ends in divorce in England and Wales lasts 13 years. That's 13 years of his surname going onto everything: your payslip, mortgage, pension, school forms, prescriptions, loyalty cards and logins. Most people have 25 to 40 places to update.

So you change the big ones, run out of energy in the middle of everything else going on, and stop. Then the letters keep coming. In a 2026 Opinium poll, 63% of divorced Brits still had their married name.

You shouldn't have to chase this on your own. That's what we're here for.

What you'll usually need

Your documents, depending on where you are

Divorce final

You have your final order

You usually don't need a deed poll. Your final order (or decree absolute), marriage certificate and birth certificate are normally enough.

Divorce not final yet

Still going through it

You can still go back to your name now. You'll need a deed poll as proof until your final order comes through.

Worth knowing

A few things people worry about

A different surname from your children

It's legal, common, and schools and GPs deal with it every day. When you travel abroad without their dad, carry their birth certificate and a consent letter.

There's no time limit

Whether your divorce came through last month or ten years ago, you can go back to your name whenever you're ready.

Mrs to Ms

Your title isn't part of your legal name. You can change it whenever you like, for free. Just tell the places that use it.

Questions

Do I need a deed poll to go back to my maiden name?

Not usually, once your divorce is final. Your final order, marriage certificate and birth certificate are normally enough. If your divorce isn't final yet, you'll need a deed poll. More on deed polls.

Why can't I start with my passport?

HM Passport Office asks for evidence that you're already using your previous name, such as a payslip or a letter from your council, plus a signed statement that you've gone back to it for all purposes. So your employer and council come first. See GOV.UK.

What does "done for you" actually mean?

We find every organisation that has your married name, prepare each letter and form, and keep track until they're all changed. Where an organisation lets us contact them for you, we do. Where they need you, such as the Passport Office and DVLA, we get everything ready so you just sign and post.

I'd rather do it myself. Can I?

Of course. Our free checklist gives you every place to update, what each one needs, and the order to do them in.

What is the Old Name Check?

A new check we're piloting. It looks for your married name on the public web, such as wedding websites, photographer blogs, people-finder sites, old work pages, Companies House and public social profiles, then sorts what it finds into what we can remove for you, what only you can change, and what can't be removed. It only searches for the person who signed up, after checking their ID. Join the pilot.

Is it OK to keep my married name?

Yes. Lots of women keep it to match their children, or because their career is built on it. That's a real choice, and if you change your mind later, there's no time limit.

How long does it take?

Most places update within days or a few weeks. The passport takes longest. Done in the right order, most people finish in a couple of months, instead of finding stragglers for years.
