Common questions

The UK rules, in plain English

Do I need a Deed Poll after getting married?

Not if you're taking your partner's surname or double-barrelling both surnames, because your Marriage Certificate (or civil partnership certificate) is enough. You need one for a new combined surname or a first name change. Read more.

What is the difference between a Deed Poll and a Marriage Certificate?

A Marriage Certificate is the official record that links your names, so most organisations accept it as proof. A Deed Poll is a document you sign to declare a name change that no certificate covers. Which one you need depends on the name you're moving to.

What do I need to go back to my previous name after divorce?

Usually your Final Order (called a Decree Absolute before April 2022) together with your Marriage Certificate, which shows how the names connect. Each organisation sets its own rules, and your checklist shows what each one asks for. More on going back.

Can I use a Deed Poll to go back instead?

Yes, some people prefer one, and it's useful if you don't have your divorce papers to hand. An unenrolled Deed Poll needs two witnesses aged 18 or over. Read the guide.

Does it cost anything to change my name?

Changing your name is free with almost every organisation. The main cost is a new passport (£102 online or £115.50 by post), paid directly to HM Passport Office. Our checklist is free too.

Is the checklist only for marriage?

No. It also covers going back to a previous name after a divorce, and changing your name for any other reason.
